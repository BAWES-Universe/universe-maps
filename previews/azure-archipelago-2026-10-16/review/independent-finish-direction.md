## What the references actually contribute

- Tea Garden: a convincing setting surrounds the paths. Irregular planted edges, rocks, water, terraces and the furnished pavilion form distinct spaces. Curving paths and short bridges reveal destinations. Detail clusters at edges and activity areas; walking surfaces remain legible.
- Volcanic Observatory: two strong architectural destinations and a bridge that reads as a supported structure. Cliff faces, parapets, piers, threshold posts and consistent shadows explain elevation. Its plaza is deliberately open, but patterned paving and a building frontage give that openness purpose. Borrow this structural clarity, not lava, gothic towers or dark palette.
- Sky Village: the terminal is a recognizable landmark. Buildings, canopy, planters and terraces form a hierarchy. Bridges have convincing landings and connect places that have distinct purposes. A coastal pavilion or glass conservatory can supply the same navigational clarity without copying an airship terminal.
- Bazaar: a central landmark and coherent perimeter activity frame a clear circulation field. Repeated forms vary through lighting, contents and grouped decoration. Borrow organized edge activity and material transitions, not stalls everywhere or indiscriminate clutter.

## Concrete deficiencies and improvements

1. **Lounge is the largest gap.** The cutaway view shows a giant marble rectangle bounded by three teal bars, two table pairs and three isolated rear chairs. It reads as a furniture test room. Give it a recognizable coastal salon: articulated wall bays/pilasters, glazed framing, a continuous low rear console or reading ledge, restrained shelves/ceramics, and two small grouped seating zones on flush inset floor panels. Keep the central circulation field empty. Architecture should remain understandable with the canopy hidden. Keep rear chair bodies clear of walls; do not fill the newly repaired approaches.
2. **Hub lacks spatial organization.** The fountain and four duplicated table pairs sit independently on broad uniform marble. Use a coherent flush wave/compass mosaic around the fountain, narrow axial paving bands that visibly lead to bridge mouths, and understated inset panels under seating groups. Add actual parapet/landing detail around the perimeter instead of flat solid-color border bars. Do not add more chairs merely to occupy space.
3. **Bridge setting is too flat.** Repeated floor strips and rails need paired end piers, a continuous parapet coping, consistent sidewall depth/shadow, and a short intentional threshold band where bridge paving meets island paving. Keep every bridge's full256px/8-tile clear corridor and all8 mouth lanes. Put piers, planters and lanterns outside that clear width. Existing viewing bays should read as supported cantilevered overlooks. Avoid new elevation illusions or stair art on a flat walkable route unless the native map can represent them clearly.
4. **Ocean has excessive visual weight.** Bright, high-contrast caustic repetition competes with furniture and shoreline detail. Keep visible restrained motion, but lower offshore contrast and distinguish shallow coastal turquoise from slightly deeper quieter blue farther out. Preserve irregular rocky/beach edges and continuous water; avoid bringing back square island backgrounds or thin visible atlas seams. Any recoloring should be evaluated at normal Woka scale, not just an attractive zoomed-out overview.
5. **Garden is the strongest current island but repetitive.** Four identical square planters and loose individual chairs make its center formal and generic. Retain the now-usable west overlooks and beach; vary planting species/height/color within existing footprints, add a few restrained flush paving accents or a planted low boundary at the margins, and strengthen the fountain's relation to surrounding paths. Do not obstruct the repaired west necks or the narrow sand route beside rocks.
6. **Café is the most convincing occupied destination.** Keep its service counter and varied seating. A small coherent canopy/backbar treatment and subtler localized counter detail will improve it more than additional scattered furniture. Preserve the staff gap, balcony access, and native chair/table spacing.
7. **Gathering space has a giant emblem but little architectural purpose.** Keep its open center for people. Make the shell motif a flush stone/mosaic inlay with less bevel/specular contrast; its current gold relief looks raised despite being walkable. Frame the gathering field with discreet perimeter architecture or grouped lounge details, while retaining the distinctive lobed overlooks. Do not replace the open area with tables or a quest apparatus.

## Proposed hub judgment

The proposal is directionally good, not final-quality acceptance. Its shell-and-wave mosaic supplies a focal hierarchy, four axial routes, and visually grounded seating bays; these directly address the current hub's emptiness without adding collision clutter. Keep the center clearly walkable outside the fountain.

Reservations: the huge circular motif occupies most of the image; its dense micro-mosaic and heavy marble veining could become noisy at the final768px hub size. The worn sandy stone also differs from Azure's current polished ivory surfaces. Reduce peripheral micro-detail, keep one shared ivory/teal/brass material palette, and let the central mosaic dominate only this hub rather than repeating an equally large emblem on every island. The image remains a square flat floor without perimeter architecture, supported edges, bridge thresholds or contextual ocean; it cannot by itself match Tea Garden/Observatory spatial finish. Its fountain aspect, furniture spacing and exact anchors differ from installed art, so it is not a safe drop-in under existing collision or seats.

Recommended order: establish one shared material/shore/bridge vocabulary; finish hub and lounge architectural identity; refine the already stronger café/garden/gathering; then judge normal-scale full gameplay views again. Keep Woka/camera scale unchanged and restore native collision/seat regression after any art that moves props. Performance optimization is valuable but does not close these visual gaps.
